A solo exploration game

ASHFALL: Signal Zero

Kessa, a dying moon, has gone quiet — except for one signal that shouldn't exist. Climb its cracked highlands, tune your relic to the frequency of the dead, and find out who is still calling home.

See what's inside

What you'll actually be doing

Tune the relic, read the moon

Your signal relic is the only tool you carry. Rotate its three rings to isolate a frequency, and the terrain around you responds — hidden paths hum, broken machines flicker awake, and the moon's old wounds glow through the rock.

Survive the cold hours

Kessa's nights drop fast. Shelter, ration warmth, and choose which climbs are worth the risk before dark.

Four regions, one story

The Glassfields, the Hollow Spires, the Undertide, and the Ashfall crater itself — each rewrites what you thought the signal meant.

A companion who remembers

Ren, a scavenger drone, keeps a log of every choice you make and will bring it up later — sometimes kindly, sometimes not.

No combat, no health bar

Ashfall is built around reading, listening, and deciding. Tension comes from what you might miss, not what might kill you.

Why we're building Ashfall

We kept making exploration games where the reward for curiosity was a bigger fight. Ashfall started as a question: what if the reward was just understanding something a little better than you did an hour ago?

There is combat-free, so all of the game's tension is spent on pacing, sound, and the slow reveal of Kessa's last transmission. It's a small game. We'd rather it be sharp than long.

What's in the current build

  • Story Mode — the full signal, start to end, roughly 4–6 hours.
  • Wander Mode — no story triggers, just Kessa to explore at your own pace.
  • Archive Mode — replay any decoded transmission you've already found.
  • Photo Mode — pause anywhere and frame a shot of the moon.
